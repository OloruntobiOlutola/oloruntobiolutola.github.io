Beyond Abstracts: Learning Scientific Paper Embeddings from Full-Text Windows

Younes Djemmal, Oloruntobi Olutola, Kim Gerdes · Questel / LISN, Université Paris-Saclay

Embedding models for scientific papers are usually trained on titles and abstracts only. We trained encoders (SciBERT, ModernBERT) contrastively on citation pairs, using random windows of the papers' full text, and built the held-out citation-retrieval benchmark used to evaluate them. Every trained variant beats SPECTER2 by +10 to +12.5 NDCG@10 on title-and-abstract retrieval, and the full-text models lead clearly when queries come from the body text. I designed the benchmark and the evaluation pipeline, and found and fixed the evaluation artefacts that had been inflating scores.

Estimating Varietal Proportions in Wheat Grain Mixtures with Fine-Tuned Deep Vision Models

O. Ali Houmed, Q.-P. Ho, A. Moujar, O. P. Olutola, G. D. M. Okou, R. Lu, T. G. H. Khuong, M. Ecarnot, S. Chevallier, T. Flutre, L. Sun-Hosoya, F. P. Landes · Université Paris-Saclay, LISN, INRIA, INRAE, DATAIA

Sowing several wheat varieties in one field supports agroecology, but at harvest nobody knows how much each variety contributed. We released a public dataset of 26,882 RGB images of single grains (8 varieties, 2 growing seasons) and compared fine-tuned vision models (ResNet, EfficientNetV2, ViT, Swin, among others). They reach up to 94% accuracy and estimate each variety's share of the harvest to within 5 percentage points. RGB images alone are enough, and about 1,600 samples per class suffice. A model trained on one year drops to about 35% accuracy on the next, so the domain shift between years is severe. Fine-tuning the whole network (94%) clearly beats retraining only the head (89%).

Scientific Literature for Patent Search: Citation Graph, Citation Embeddings & Statement Extraction

Qatent AI Lab (Questel group) · May – Aug 2026 · Supervised by Kim Gerdes

Patent prior-art search has a blind spot: much of the relevant evidence is in scientific papers, not other patents. The lab's goal is one retrieval and RAG system that reasons over patents and science together. I built the data and model foundations for it: a citation graph covering the whole scientific record, citation-trained paper embeddings, and LLM pipelines that pull citation and contribution statements out of papers.

528Mwork records merged across sources
3.08Bdeduplicated citation edges
+12.5NDCG@10 over SPECTER2
33Katomic statements from 100 papers

01 The citation graph and data foundation

I merged Semantic Scholar, S2ORC and OpenAlex into one consistent corpus, even though the raw data is larger than the available disk.

My contribution
  • A SQL crosswalk of identifiers (DOI, arXiv, PMID…) maps every paper to one stable ID. The bulk content lives in partitioned Parquet, and the biggest joins run in hash-partitioned buckets with DuckDB.
  • Delivered 528M merged records, 164M deduplicated abstracts and a 3.08-billion-edge citation graph, filtered to paper-to-paper links only.
  • A seven-stage update pipeline that can be safely re-run and resumed. New releases are added without breaking any existing ID.
Finding: fusing two different papers into one is the most damaging error at this scale, so I built explicit checks to detect false merges, plus row-count checks after every stage.

02 Citation-trained paper embeddings

Encoders (SciBERT, ModernBERT) fine-tuned with a contrastive loss on citation pairs, so that a paper sits close to the papers it cites.

My contribution
  • Built the held-out citation-retrieval benchmark and a separate embed-then-evaluate pipeline, with baselines run through the same code.
  • Found and fixed self-retrieval inflation (papers retrieving parts of themselves) and a mismatch between how training and evaluation sampled each paper's text.
Finding: every trained model beats SPECTER2 in all three retrieval settings: title-abstract 0.544 vs 0.419, body-to-body 0.457 vs 0.192. Full-text windows win on body-based queries. Published at CORIA-TALN 2026.

03 Citation & contribution statements

Toward an "auto-reviewer": extracting the claims a paper attributes to the works it cites, and the contributions it claims for itself.

My contribution
  • An 11-label rhetorical taxonomy that adds method and citation labels missing from prior work. A corpus of 10,000 papers across 23 fields (1.82M sentences), labelled by an LLM panel (4 models, 3 providers) with a separate judge model.
  • Five encoder classifiers distilled from those labels (macro-F1 0.697–0.742), small enough to label the entire literature.
  • Contribution extraction in two stages: a cheap span finder (works on over 70% of papers), then LLM refinement (F1 ≈ 0.77 against a model-written reference). Papers get the full text only when the model asks for more context.
Finding: the model ranking flips when the reference changes. On human labels, 110M-parameter SciBERT matches 396M ModernBERT-large at about 5× the throughput. Differences usually credited to model size were really differences between training corpora.

04 Whole papers → atomic, verifiable statements

A pipeline that turns a full paper into self-contained, labelled single claims, each traceable to its source sentences.

My contribution
  • Overlapping chunks: each chunk gives the model earlier text as read-only context but extracts only from its own sentences, so it can resolve references without ever producing a duplicate.
  • Three pipeline designs behind one interface, run on a local 35B mixture-of-experts model served with vLLM.
  • Every citation marker is checked against the source text, so any the model invents are flagged.
Finding: 100 papers produced 33,030 statements with none left unlabelled, and 90.4% of citation markers were verified in the source. Batching cut model calls 15-fold, turning an unfinishable job into a 15-hour run.

AI agents · Full-stack AI product

AI Career Operating System

A career assistant built from AI agents. It parses your CV into a structured profile, pulls real job postings, ranks them by semantic fit, and then rewrites your CV and drafts a cover letter for each role you pick.

  • A workflow engine runs specialised agents: profile analysis, semantic matching, CV optimisation and ATS analysis, and cover-letter generation.
  • Semantic job matching with embeddings in Qdrant. Filters on date and seniority run inside the vector search, and domain scoring rules re-rank the results.
  • Swappable providers: the LLM can be Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter and the embeddings OpenAI or local FastEmbed/BGE, each chosen with one setting.
  • Hexagonal (ports and adapters) architecture with a dependency-free domain layer. Job sources are added as plug-ins (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby).
  • Responsible by design: no scraping that breaks a site's terms, and no made-up facts. Visa, salary and similar features stay off until a licensed data source is connected.

Information retrieval · Challenge

Hybrid Citation Recommendation

Given a paper, find the papers it should cite in a 20,000-document corpus. I built a hybrid retrieval pipeline that combines sparse models (BM25L, TF-IDF) with dense ones (BGE-large, SPECTER2, MiniLM) through nested reciprocal rank fusion. On top of that, it routes each query to the models that work best for its research field, drops models that are weak in that field, and expands queries with pseudo-relevance feedback.

NDCG@10: 0.52 → 0.76 on validation · 0.73 on the held-out Codabench leaderboard

Trustworthy AI · Research paper (team of 4)

Political Bias and Temporal Dynamics in LLMs

Most studies measure political bias in LLMs once, as if models never change. We tracked it across model generations within the same families (e.g. LLaMA, GPT), combining measures of what models say with the sentiment they express. The goal was to see how changes to safety data and alignment shift political stance over time.

ACL-format paper · bias measured across model generations, not a single snapshot
